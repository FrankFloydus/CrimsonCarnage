- Port the single-player workflow. Ability logic and execution calculations live in GDScript. Author ability and effect definitions in `.ability.json` and `.effect.json`. Godot loads the JSON into native definition objects for gameplay. Keep definitions, grant specs, active casts, outgoing effect specs and active effect handles distinct.
- Saved AbilityDefinition and GameplayEffectDefinition IDs share one namespace of unique positive integers. Zero is unset. Keep gameplay tags, grants, casts, and active effect handles in their own fields. Validate authored definitions together to detect ID collisions.
- Register canonical gameplay tags in the standalone Ability Editor's **Tags** tab. `tags/gameplay_tags.json` is the project registry consumed by the Godot Inspector picker and headless authoring validation. Assignments use explicitly registered names. Matching fields can also query implied parents. Keep lifecycle phase keys and animation events separate from gameplay tags.
- Start with the actor's existing AbilitySystem, its authored definitions, and its owned runtime instances. Use an AbilityDefinition for a reusable action and a GameplayEffectDefinition for a status or gameplay consequence. Route player and AI through the same activation API. Let execution delegate physics, collision, and animation to the components that own them.
- Keep the input gesture on the ability definition and default keybindings on player Input Map actions. The player router selects a grant from the starting loadout and forwards button-up to the exact accepted cast. Keep delivery timing on the execution and route casting visuals through the actor's cue bindings.
- Author saved abilities with [Phased Ability Execution](../docs/ability_composition.md). Choose a target query, a delivery, and ordered actions or reactions. Keep per-cast phase and delivery state on the cast and retained behavior state on the effect handle. Use the effect's damage execution and its Amount for authored damage.
- The [standalone editor](../tools/ability_editor/README.md#create-and-edit-definitions) writes combat JSON directly and uses Godot's exported field metadata for its forms. Keep inactive stored fields and shared effect references intact. Author a shared hit as one **Apply Effects** action in Delivered Hit mode. Confirmed-hit and actual-Health-damage follow-ups are native reactions.
- Prototype actions are direct grants. Their damage and timing come from the authored effects and animations. Keep equipment authoring out of the ASC Inspector. Dedicated weapon formula tests can supply a runtime source, which the ASC validates before committing cost and snapshots for detached hits. Future equipment can grant abilities through the same API.
- An effect publishes actor state through counted Granted Tags. The consuming ability owns required tags and puts its **Activation Blockers** and **Cancel On Tags** on its Interruption Policy. Configure the start and active-action rules separately. Keep the effect independent of the actions that react to it.
- For a condition-driven response, use [Gameplay Events](../docs/ability_system.md#gameplay-events). Producers send a fact and payload through `AbilitySystem.send_gameplay_event()`. Ordinary grants can declare an activation event. Active executions handle later events and decide when to finish. Keep facts separate from effect-granted state and animation timing. For a comparison between two final actor attributes, reuse the authored `AttributeGameplayEventRule` under Attributes. Avoid responses that reverse their own trigger predicate.
- For an indefinite effect owned by an activation, use the `AbilityMaintainEffectAction` in a phase. The definition owns the effect reference, the cast owns the independent handle, and the effect runtime owns its tag and modifier contributions. The action restores the handle after a clear and releases it on cancellation, revocation, death, and synchronous interruption during application.
- **Encumbered.** The Player's carry rule reports enter and exit events. The ordinarily granted Encumbered action listens to them and maintains the effect that grants only `State.Encumbered`. Sprint uses the state in its policy's Activation Blockers and Cancel On Tags. Movement computes the numeric penalties and UI observes state and live results.
- Use direct effect application for a standalone data operation with an existing owner, such as actor initialization or delivery of a resolved hit. An effect-driven reaction may trigger a separate granted action where the reaction owns behavior. Choose the direction based on the action's lifecycle and identify the owner before adding a new field or node.
- Author ability conflicts through owned and blocked state tags. Current animated attacks and spells own and block on `State.CombatAction`. Track shared animation playback by the activation that started it. Playback ownership is separate from activation policy and from UI classification. Route release, interruption, cancellation and cleanup to the owning activation or effect handle.
- Author one-time payments as Resource Costs and cooldowns as Charge Pools. A single charge with a recharge duration is an ordinary cooldown. Commit through the active cast at the policy's configured phase. Sprint owns a single Infinite GE_StaminaUpkeep while active. Author its Period and calculation's Stamina per Interval on the effect. Movement measures actual grounded travel, retains unpaid time and reports when a charge is due. A paid charge that would leave less than the next full charge spends the remainder, triggering exhaustion at zero.
- A Resource Percent cost with `use_payment_pool` reads the payer's live selected payment pool using the Amount's Current, Maximum or Missing basis. Keep the link on the cost so one Amount can serve multiple pools. Definitions without the flag use the Amount's authored basis pool, actor and timing.

## Live combat rules

- The live game is clean Combat 2.0. Do not restore any Combat 1.0 rule, attribute or example by assumption.
- The catalogue starts with thirty-one protected built-in attributes. The Ability Editor's **Attributes** tab authors metadata and additional generic attributes in `attributes/attributes.json`. `attributes/combat_attributes.gd` owns built-in engine bindings and consumes the registry for active IDs, names and Inspector choices. Custom IDs start at 100 and stay reserved after deletion. Health stays at 0, None at -1 and retired slots remain absent from authoring.
- GE_DefaultAttributes owns starting values and GE_AttributeDerivations owns the five live derived-base modifiers. Progression, bounds and combat formula tuning belong in `combat_rules.json`. The Accuracy Rating edit view authors the existing two level-gap arrays in that file. Godot owns hit-chance evaluation and supplies the editor's draft preview. Keep one authoring owner per value.
- Full primary values contribute flat stats: Strength grants 2 Physical Power and 1 Accuracy for Brutality, plus 2 Carry Capacity; Dexterity grants 2 Physical Power and 1 Accuracy for Finesse; Intellect grants 2 Magic Power, 5 Mana and 1 spell Accuracy; Vitality grants 10 Health and 5 Stamina. Use one governing primary, never an automatic Strength+Dexterity sum.
- Context tags select the action: Weapon.Finesse, Weapon.Brutality, Ability.Spell. Ambiguous weapon contexts do not guess a primary. Accuracy Rating resolves the same level-based success check for attacks and spells, independently of damage type.
- The implemented [2.0 Accuracy curve](../docs/combat_system_2_0.md#accuracy-and-levels) has base 80% at 10 Accuracy/equal level, 100% attainable through +5, explicit ceiling 50% at +6 and 10% at +10 (base 8%). Rating scale is 20 + 2*(attacker level-1) + 10*max(gap,0); rating progress is clamp((Accuracy-10)/scale,-1,1), interpolating base to ceiling. Numeric arrays/constants belong in combat_rules.json; public CombatMath probabilities are fractions 0..1. Preserve the document's lower/higher-level continuation; do not borrow the 1.0 curves or add level-damage multipliers.
- One hostile Accuracy check gates eligible damage and attached effects, including modifier-only hostile control payloads. A failed check emits miss or resist and applies no payload. Confirmed contact, immunity, deflection, reflection and actual Health loss are separate outcomes. Modifier-only self delivery and Ability.Beneficial ally delivery bypass the check. Direct effect application is the explicit non-hit API. Weapon, Accuracy and critical random streams are independent. Inspection consumes no rolls.
- Health/Mana/Stamina Regen default to 0/5/10 points/sec, nonnegative and uncapped. GE_ResourceRegeneration owns periodic execution. The calculation samples live rates and skips Stamina only while `State.Stamina.RecoveryBlocked` is present. Sprint and Stamina Exhaustion grant it for their respective lifetimes. A positive-to-zero Stamina crossing sends `Event.Stamina.Depleted`. Transient cleanup and death preserve startup derivation and recovery handles. Recovery is inhibited while dead.
- Evaluate owned modifiers as (base+flat) × combined increased × independent More. Preserve serialized operation IDs. Set Base explicitly overrides the full derived foundation; ordinary gear uses Add Flat.
- Critical Chance starts at 0%, receives no primary/level contribution and clamps to 0–100% after normal modifiers. Critical Damage starts at 0 bonus percentage points, is nonnegative and uncapped. Shared JSON tuning owns bounds and base_critical_multiplier (1.5); total critical multiplier is 1.5 + CriticalDamage/100. Add Flat 50 therefore gives 200% total damage. Roll once only after a damaging hit passes Accuracy, using both critical stats in the source action context. Share result/multiplier across all damage components; attached modifiers are unchanged. Modifier-only payloads cannot crit. Direct non-hit apply_effect never rolls implicitly. A new delivered context resets prior critical state. Preserve independent seeded streams and detached source fallbacks; inspection and failed Accuracy consume no critical roll. Criticals bypass no defenses: multiply raw source damage before Armor/penetration or matching resistance. Damage telemetry retains pre-critical raw magnitude, critical metadata and actual Health loss; count critical hits once from hit_outcome, not once per damage component. F3 exposes both attributes in percentage units; floating critical numbers and F2 show the result.
- Movement Speed is a bonus percentage with a 0% default and −100%/+25% bounds. Identity 54 remains live and 27 stays retired. Clamp after ordinary modifiers. Normal Run Speed in Movement Settings defines the physical 4 m/s baseline. Apply the bonus, one gait multiplier and the encumbrance factor. F3 shows the bonus percentage. Keep locomotion responsibilities and authoring in player/AGENTS.md.
- Attack Speed and Cast Speed start at 0% and cap at +100% effective bonus. Author ordinary bonuses with Add Flat percentage points. Sample action-scoped speed once in the active cast and apply 1 + effective bonus/100 to playback. Attacks use Attack Speed and can first normalize an animation to the weapon's authored cycle. Spells use Cast Speed. Release and recovery follow the animation. The authored method key controls release and the cast bar reads playback progress to that same key. Keep cooldown independent.
- Armor defaults to 0 rating, nonnegative and uncapped through the normal modifier pipeline with no primary/level contribution. CombatMath.get_armor_reduction converts it to min(C, A/(A+K)), where C is armor_max_reduction (0.75), T is armor_rating_at_cap (1000) and K = T*(1-C)/C. Keep these numeric values in JSON. Reduction caps at 75% from 1000 rating; do not clamp the underlying rating, so later debuffs use full overcap contributions. No hit-size or level factor. Only Physical damage uses Armor; Magic uses school resistance, never both on one component. Modifier potency/Accuracy are unaffected.
- Armor Penetration defaults to 0% with no primary/level contribution. Apply source flat/increased/more modifiers, then clamp to 0–100% using shared JSON bounds. Query it from the source in each Physical component's canonical context. Effective Armor is full modified target Armor × (1 − source penetration/100), then use the Armor curve. Never clip target Armor to 1000 first, subtract penetration from reduction percentage, mutate target Armor or use the defender's penetration. Magic, Accuracy and retained modifier potency are unaffected. Cache scoped source values for detached delivery as usual. Telemetry keeps armor as full target rating and reports effective Armor and source penetration separately. F3 percentage units include penetration; it is not a resistance.
- Resistances start at 0%, receive no primary/level contribution and clamp to −100%..+80% only after the modifier pipeline. Their base/bounds are shared JSON tuning. Preserve negative values and underlying overcap contributions; Add Flat uses percentage points. Increased/More multiply the signed value (−50 with 20% increased becomes −60). Movement Speed retains its signed −100% to +25% range. Other noncurrent stats retain their documented nonnegative/capacity rules.
- GameplayEffectDefinition keeps DamageType Physical0/Magic1; a separate MagicSchool enum selects Fire/Cold/Lightning/Void/Necrotic, defaulting to Fire for older untyped Magic effects. Physical ignores that field. All schools use Magic Power. Derive canonical damage-type/school tags from fields and reject conflicting effect tags. Do not reinterpret old invalid damage-type numbers as new schools.
- Magic damage uses its matching school resistance. For a direct Magic component of a spell hit, subtract nonnegative, uncapped source Spell Penetration points from the target's final resistance, with a −100% effective floor. Spell Penetration defaults to zero, uses component-scoped source values, and applies only to that hit. Keep the target stat, Accuracy, critical rules and other delivery types independent. Telemetry preserves target and effective resistance separately.
- The live WeaponDefinition owns family, physical min/max and authored base cycle. Finesse: one-handed dagger, one-/two-handed sword and bow. Brutality: one-/two-handed mace and axe. The earlier ritual dagger, scepter, staff and focus names remain in the live casting-only catalogue. The designed Shield weapon type is not yet present in the live catalogue. No implicits.
- The current design replaces those caster families with Orb of Power (elemental), Talisman (Void), Relic (necromancy/Necrotic) and future Fetish (minions). All ten Relic visuals are one type with one shared tree, not separate gameplay variants. The design makes every weapon type unique-equipped and progression shared per character/type across item replacements. None of this catalogue/equipment/tree work is implemented; do not silently add it during unrelated tasks. Physical trees modify basic attacks/combos; caster trees can grant active abilities. Selected Perk behavior remains a working proposal.
- Shield is a defensive weapon type in the current design, restricted to the off-hand on the left arm. It follows unique-equipped and shared character/type progression, with its own tree enhancing blocking. Its classification does not assign a governing primary, damage, shield stats or block formula. Shield catalogue support, slot enforcement, blocking and its tree remain unimplemented.
- The accepted caster-weapon Power-range/Casting Speed extension is design-only; see [Magic damage and casting](../docs/combat_system_2_0.md#magic-damage-and-casting). The live character Cast Speed bonus is separate from the proposed weapon-specific factor and Power fields. The old main-weapon/Focus clock and independent bare-handed spell model are no longer agreed design. Keep implemented facts distinct from pending rules; domains do not restore Combat 1.0 attributes or ailments.
- Physical weapon damage is one stable min/max roll plus applicable Physical Power × base cycle, with authored contribution sharing when needed. Snapshot weapon values into the cast; projectile travel or extra holding never increases the coefficient.
- The animation method track owns the release frame. The shared projectile execution waits for that event, prepares the outgoing specs and hands them to the projectile. Keep damage and its execution calculation in the impact effect. Fireball authors a camera trace distance and a scene Marker3D launch origin.
- Gameplay Effects author duration policy, period, tag requirements, modifiers, calculations and presentation. Retained effects own counted tags and modifier handles. Use the effect spec for per-application data. The framework supports periodic execution and stacking policies. Fireball's authored impact is instant.
- The live attribute catalogue omits Dodge, Block, Absorption, Healing Power, attack/cast speed ratings and cooldown-recovery stats. Effects can author prevention, absorption and healing using the composable runtime. The saved Playground grants Fireball as its combat action. Acquired Health Regen remains an approved recovery source. Movement Settings own encumbrance tuning. F3 supplies test weight and the character sheet displays load against capacity.
- Keep resource capacity changes separate from resource restoration. Current pools clamp to capacity; capacity increases never refill. Test Restore/reset APIs are debug actions, not a consumable system.
- AbilitySystem uses its explicit valid attributes reference; if empty at startup, resolve only the actor sibling named Attributes, never create or duplicate a component. Keep the export tooltip and tests clear about this default. Missing source/target attributes in CombatTestTools.refresh_matchup must produce a diagnostic rather than a nil get_value call.
- Author starting actions in the actor's Granted Abilities list. The Player scene lists Sprint, Jump, Crouch, Encumbered, Stamina Exhaustion, Fireball and Test Ability. Each grant has one active use at a time. Death and teardown cancel all active instances. F3 selects Attack and Spell definitions. The combat recorder excludes movement actions and keeps status activations out of F1 statistics.
- Ability Icon is an optional Texture2D reference on AbilityDefinition. The standalone Ability Editor reads its project path from JSON for the catalog thumbnail.
- Effect Icon and HUD Category are authored presentation fields. The Ability Editor uses the Icon for its Effects catalog. The HUD tracks retained instances by handle and shows configured buffs on the left and debuffs on the right above the bars.
- Ability and effect JSON produces shared native definitions and executions for each run. Weapon definitions remain native authored resources. Keep clocks, mutable cast data, effect instances and telemetry internal. Player and AI share activation.
- Gameplay tags remain generic counted authoring infrastructure. Default action/death tags do not introduce retired control-status mechanics.
- The saved combat panel has Player, Dummy and Statistics tabs. F3 uses searchable sliders and a computed character sheet. Use whole steps for level and primaries, and 0.01 for fractional values. Speed override sliders start at 0–100% and can expand to test overcap values. The sheet shows effective percentages, hit chance, normal-hit damage, physical reduction and total Critical Damage. Keep raw rating and bonus inputs in tooltips. F1 uses Statistics and F2 toggles the log. The saved HUD follows live resources and retained effects. Fireball's cast bar follows its active animation and disappears at release or cancellation.
- Keep formula verification separate from itemization. Fixture weapon values are tests, not approved item budgets or enemy content. Randomized bonuses need no enforced exchange rate.
- The Python combat simulator and its reports use an explicit frozen pre-2.0 model and cannot validate live 2.0 gameplay. Preserve historical evidence; never mix its results with current verification.
- Every exported property must have meaningful `##` tooltip documentation, including units and scope. Update the focused system documents when behavior changes.

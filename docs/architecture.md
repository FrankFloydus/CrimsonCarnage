# Project architecture

## Project folders

| Folder | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `data/items/` | Editable `ItemDefinition` resources |
| `data/player/` | Movement, look, and crouch tuning |
| `data/melee/` | Authored attack reach tuning |
| `data/effects/` | Shared blood splatter style |
| `scenes/` | Levels, player, characters, weapons, effect coordinators, and UI |
| `scripts/` | Player, melee, effect, item, and UI code in separate domain folders |
| `particles/` | Reusable particle scenes, including the directional blood burst |
| `materials/`, `shaders/`, `textures/`, `meshes/` | Standalone rendering assets |

Place new data-driven definitions under `data/<category>/`. Use type folders such as `particles/`, `textures/`, and `meshes/` when adding those assets. [Folder conventions](godot_conventions.md#folder-organization)

## Scenes and ownership

`project.godot` starts the Playground. The scene owns the ground, parkour blocks, arena collision boundaries, lighting, and environment. It instances the Player, two stair courses, crouch tunnels, the blood test target and effect coordinator, and the read-only item catalog inspector. The gray floor and blood-test backstop use **25 cm** charcoal grid cells. Parkour surfaces and the blood-test barrel share the darker grid material with **12.5 cm** cells. A gray sky, broad directional light, and distance fog give the course its overcast lighting. [Project settings](../project.godot) · [Playground scene](../scenes/levels/playground.tscn) · [Grid materials](../materials/prototype)

The Player is a `CharacterBody3D` with a standing capsule **1.80 m** high and **0.35 m** in radius. The body origin is at the feet, and the `Look` pivot places the standing eye at **1.62 m**. [Player scene](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

| Owner | Responsibility | Settings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Player | Samples the local input scope and resolves stance and gait before each movement step | [Player controller](../scripts/player/player.gd) |
| Controls | Authors the Framework bindings used by both playable player scenes | [Input Layout](../data/player/default_input_layout.tres) |

## Dynamic Sky addon

West Ridge Observatory instances the Dynamic Sky rig. The rig resolves global and local weather, drives the atmosphere and celestial lights, and forwards conditions to precipitation, spatial audio, and weather-responsive surfaces. The Volumetric renderer and the native directional light projector share the cloud field, with cheaper projection paths for 2D Dynamic and Static. A distant lightning controller reports the strike position for the localized sky flash and travel-time-delayed thunder. The Playground uses its own environment settings. The UDW comparison scene instances the same Dynamic Sky rig with a reflected reference camera, test geometry, and weather-responsive checker materials. Its capture controls belong to the comparison scene root. [UDW comparison](../addons/dynamic_sky/demo/udw_comparison/README.md) [Authoring and runtime API](dynamic_sky.md) · [Unreal reference map](ultra_dynamic_sky_audit.md)

Radial Storm actors participate in the same-world weather sampler and own their horizontal footprint and travel. Their precipitation easing uses the weather resolved so far and the storm's target. Material receivers use separate linear accumulation inputs while the rain impact channel follows the resolved rain. In Volumetric mode, the rig sends the ordered storm footprints in rig-relative kilometers to the shared cloud field. The visible sky, native shadow projector, and in-cloud fog evaluate the same coverage. The actor owns its depth-tested exterior precipitation shaft, obscured flash, and distant thunder bed. These components resolve weather and listener state from the Dynamic Sky rig in their World3D. The optional root scheduler selects a seasonal profile and starts the same saved actor and lifecycle used for manual authoring. [Radial Storm authoring](dynamic_sky.md#radial-storm) · [Weather sampler](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/weather_state.gd) · [Scheduler](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/radial_storm_scheduler.gd)

The main DynamicSky script owns effect authoring: typed resources configure rain, snow, airborne haze, screen weather, optional camera shake and debris, audio, lightning, rainbow optics, and cloud shadows. It applies them before internal child initialization and on resource changes. Optional Configuration groups the same settings; camera/listener references remain on the root. Internal components retain standalone fallbacks. WorldEnvironment remains the sole native exposed child for rendering resources. [Root effect authoring](dynamic_sky.md#author-effects-on-the-main-rig)

Weather profiles can opt into a daylight atmosphere contribution and density-limited material emission for the visible volumetric cloud pass. The rig derives the weight and atmosphere radiance from the current profile and Sun elevation. The cubemap keeps its separate lighting pass. [Profile control](../addons/dynamic_sky/dynamic_sky_weather.gd) · [Sky shader](../addons/dynamic_sky/rendering/dynamic_sky.gdshader)

**Weather Style** on Dynamic Sky selects the active profile family. Stylized profiles pair the shared weather channels with editable sky, fog, light, and particle settings. The rig dispatches rain and snow visuals to the corresponding emitter scene. Weather transitions, zones, climate, and snapshot restores operate within the selected family. [Stylized weather authoring](dynamic_sky.md#stylized-weather)

The rig's Exposure Settings resource owns observer attenuation above volumetric clouds and beneath authored water surfaces, along with fallback exposure in heavy dust and opt-in nighttime Storm calibration. The saved WeatherExposure child filters the camera's resolved local weather before the sky and camera effects consume it. Global weather, per-position physical wind, cloud motion, and material accumulation retain their unfiltered state. Saved Box or Sphere water regions define horizontal footprints and surface heights. The demo lake owns one such region. Nighttime Storm can scale global lunar and cubemap illumination while the observer's local weather controls the optional camera fallback and shadow grade. Authored camera attributes and Environment adjustments keep precedence. [Exposure authoring](dynamic_sky.md#local-weather-and-materials) · [Water Region scene](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/exposure/water_region.tscn)

The demo's terrain mesh owns meadow, trail, drainage, and forest masks in its vertex colors. Saved materials own PBR maps, physical texture scales, slope and altitude transitions, and coating appearance. A shared triplanar sampler keeps stochastic albedo, normal, and ORM transforms aligned across terrain and optional world-projected weather materials. `DynamicSkySurface` owns accumulation for explicit surface components. The root's internal material binding owns it for custom shaders with the shared weather hook, including the prototype grid. Both use the same accumulation calculation. For compatible meshes, the root overlays up to eight prioritized radial coating targets at fragment positions over the accumulated baseline. A private texture carries the field for its World3D. Rain contacts, radial coatings, and snow tracks share one runtime material copy when they use the same slot and release their own parameters independently. The fine and geological PBR bake tools derive shading maps from the original ImageGen artwork. [Terrain and surface authoring](dynamic_sky.md#local-weather-and-materials) · [Radial material field](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/radial_material_field.gd) · [Artwork provenance](dynamic_sky_imagegen.md)

Rain can render the existing falling-drop contact particles to a private viewport for nearby opaque material normals. The root's material binding supplies mesh candidates in the same World3D. The field binds the texture to compatible runtime material slots. The last effect to release a slot restores its authored assignment. [Rain authoring](dynamic_sky.md#author-effects-on-the-main-rig) · [Field controller](../addons/dynamic_sky/precipitation/rain/contact_normals/rain_contact_normal_field.gd)

An opt-in Snow Track Receiver uses the accumulated snow on its mesh and a bounded local field to displace visible vertices. A Snow Track Stamp submits accepted footfall or swept-contact marks to that receiver. The base collision continues to handle grounding. [Deep snow track authoring](dynamic_sky_snow_tracks.md)

## Movement and jumping

The player scene contains a native `MoverComponent` named `Movement` and a native `ControlsComponent` named `Controls`. Controls reads the shared input layout. The player controller selects the stance and advances the movement solver owned by `Movement` once per physics tick. Look, camera effects, and the weapon controller consume the results through the existing scene paths and signals. [Movement adapter](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd) · [Input layout](../data/player/default_input_layout.tres)

The controller reads movement input while the mouse is captured. With the mouse released, it supplies neutral input while braking, gravity, and collision continue. Movement limits diagonal input to unit length, uses separate ground and air acceleration and braking, and calls `move_and_slide()` once per physics tick. [Controller](../scripts/player/player.gd) · [Movement](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd)

| Gait | Input and stance | Configured ground speed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Run | Standing | 6 m/s |
| Walk | Standing with Ctrl held | 2 m/s |
| Crouch | Crouched | 2.4 m/s |
| Crouch walk | Crouched with Ctrl held | 1.2 m/s |

Jump works in each gait and requires floor support or a remaining **0.25 s** coyote window. The authored jump height is **1.50 m** standing and **0.75 m** crouched, with **24 m/s²** custom gravity. [Gait selection](../scripts/player/first_person_gait.gd) · [Movement tuning fields](../scripts/player/first_person_movement_settings.gd)

**Left Shift** requires directional input to spend a dash charge on the ground or in the air. Movement locks the horizontal direction from the current input and body yaw. The saved player starts with **two charges**. It restores one charge per **1.0 s** while below capacity. A burst runs at **24 m/s** for up to **0.14 s** and ends early on a blocking wall. It uses the same capsule collision and stair path as regular movement. An air dash pauses vertical motion and resumes it on exit if the player is still airborne. The burst ends with zero horizontal dash speed. Tune capacity, recharge time, speed, and duration under **Movement → Settings → Dash**. [Dash tuning fields](../scripts/player/first_person_movement_settings.gd)

## Crouch, stairs, and camera

Enter crouch while supported on a tick without a jump request. The capsule immediately changes to **1.10 m**, keeping its bottom at the feet, while the eye blends to **0.95 m** over **0.20 s**. Standing checks the full standing capsule. **Toggle** is the saved input mode and requires another press after a blocked stand. **One Shot** follows the held button and retries standing after release as clearance returns. [Crouch controller](../scripts/player/first_person_crouch.gd) · [Crouch tuning fields](../scripts/player/first_person_crouch_settings.gd)

During the crouch blend, the eye can descend early to clear a low ceiling. Moving the Player from outside the locomotion step invalidates its previous floor and stair support, clears coyote time, and resets stair camera compensation. [Crouch clearance](../scripts/player/first_person_crouch.gd) · [Movement support](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd)

The native MoverComponent uses a stair-step helper for risers up to **0.30 m** and walkable slopes up to **45°**. Floor snapping supports descent. The saved `terrain_advanced` and `terrain_reset` connections drive Look's **0.10 s** stair spring. The Player body turns for yaw, the Look pivot tilts for pitch, and Camera3D rolls with lateral movement. [Movement component](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd) · [Look controller](../scripts/player/first_person_look.gd)

Movement emits `dash_state_changed` with the locked local direction when a dash starts and signals again when it ends. **Camera3D** narrows the authored **55°** vertical FOV to **44°** on straight forward and backward dashes. The **0.80×** multiplier follows Vermintide 2's Handmaiden dash. Entry uses **0.08 s** to fit the shorter local dash, and return uses **0.30 s** from the current FOV. Both transitions use sine easing. Sideways and diagonal dashes leave any existing return blend alone. Tune the multiplier and timings under **Look → Camera3D → Dash FOV**. [Dash FOV script](../scripts/player/first_person_camera_effects.gd)

**PlayerPresentation** forwards the same signal to **DashSpeedStreaks** in every dash direction. Its original **512 × 512** flow map stores signed displacement in red and green and a tapered spoke mask in blue. Alpha feathers both toward the center. The shader samples an expanding burst and a sustained layer, then offsets the 3D scene color with their displacement. On the masked spokes, eight weighted samples taken inward along the ray stretch scene details outward. **Radial Streak Length** sets the sample span relative to the shorter viewport side. **Streak Tint** can add a separate line overlay through the same mask. Its alpha defaults to zero. The effect blends in over **0.035 s** and clears over **0.14 s** after movement stops. It draws before the vignette and lens marks, and the HUD draws above the screen effects. [Dash overlay](../scenes/effects/dash_speed_streaks.tscn) · [Shader](../shaders/effects/dash_speed_streaks.gdshader) · [Flow map](../textures/effects/dash_speed_streak_flow.png) · [Godot screen reads](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/screen-reading_shaders.html)

## Post-processing

**PostProcessing** is the final child of **PlayerPresentation → ScreenEffects**. **ScreenCopy** captures the scene after the dash, damage vignette, and lens effects have drawn. **Pass** uses the copy for a painterly filter, a continuous vignette, a screen-aligned **4 × 4 Bayer** pattern, and per-channel posterization, in that order. Applying the dither before quantization breaks up color-band boundaries. Screen effects stay at canvas layer **0**. Within the presentation scene, the ScreenEffects sibling index keeps it above the viewport's default canvas. The F4 item inspector and the damage HUD at layer **2**, including its HDR bloom composite, render above the pass. [PostProcessing scene](../scenes/effects/post_processing.tscn) · [Shader](../shaders/effects/post_processing.gdshader) · [Player scene](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

The painterly filter favors flatter neighborhoods close to the source color and groups their tones with a soft luminance curve. **Brush Stroke Strength** controls the contrast of angled paint marks. **Edge Sharpness** restores more of the source at local color boundaries. The external **Post Processing** material owns the stack settings and receives live changes through Godot's **Synchronize Scene Changes**. In the Playground, **P** calls `PlayerPresentation.toggle_painterly_preview()` to bypass the painterly filter on its own canvas item and leave the material available for live edits. The Playground owns the input and hit-test rules and calls the presentation API for the vignette, splatter, and incoming hit directions. [Material](../materials/effects/post_processing.tres) · [Playground preview](../scripts/levels/playground.gd)

The **Post Processing** editor plugin draws the saved pass in the 3D editor viewports using the active scene's material. Its preview layers are editor-only and follow the active scene. The game pass runs in the canvas after tonemapping and the gameplay overlays. Godot's Compositor callbacks run during 3D rendering, before that canvas stage. [Preview plugin](../addons/post_processing_preview/plugin.gd) · [Post-processing controls](../README.md#tune-post-processing) · [Godot Compositor](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html)

## Melee

Carnage's WeaponControl advances Crimson Framework's native melee playback and procedural rig on the physics tick. The native sweep samples the blade from the evaluated pose. Carnage's scene adapter keeps the authored clips, tuning resources, effect signals, and debug drawing. [Weapon controller](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd) · [Sweep adapter](../scripts/melee/melee_sweep_3d.gd)

The player scene starts with empty **WeaponControl** and **LeftWeaponControl** mounts on **Look → Camera3D**, and an empty **AnimationPlayer** at the player root. To equip a weapon, instance its scene under a mount and assign its **MeleeSweep** to the matching field on **WeaponControl**. Add animations to **AnimationPlayer** and assign the carry and attack clips in **WeaponControl → Animation Clips**. Key the mounts to animate their camera-local poses. [Player scene](../scenes/player/player.tscn) · [Weapon scenes](../scenes/weapons)

To add the first clip in the native Tweaker, select the empty **AnimationPlayer** in the player scene and choose **Project → Tools → Weapon Animation (Native)**. Use **Refresh Rig** if the window is open, then choose **Import Clips**. Enable the Tweaker's socket and trail overlays to inspect the imported paths. Open the [Playground](../scenes/levels/playground.tscn) and press **F6** to play the character.

In the player scene, open **Weapon Animation Tweaker** in the top editor bar after **Asset Store**. Pick a clip in the workspace header. The inspector groups the target, position, rotation, and clip motion. Use the sliders or type into the adjacent number fields. **Vertical offset**, **Width**, **Height**, and **Depth** reshape the socket paths. Positive **Camera offset** brings the motion toward the camera. **Duration** scales the keys, hit markers, and trail timing together. **Show sockets** and **Show trails** show the current mounts and draft paths with keyframe dots. The small crosshair marks the preview camera's center. The playback controls show the current time and clip duration above the timeline. The timeline snaps to key times. Scroll over it to zoom around the pointer and middle-drag to pan. Use **Fit** to see the whole clip. Hold **Ctrl** while dragging to scrub freely.

Turn on **Onion Skin** in the preview toolbar to see neighboring poses. Use its options to choose **Past**, **Future**, one to three steps, and the spacing in seconds. **Clip step** uses the animation's own sample step, like Godot's AnimationPlayer. Red marks earlier primary poses and green marks later ones. **Hide Unchanged Elements** filters out ghost meshes and joints whose transforms match the current pose.

Click **Compare**, choose a clip from the same preview rig, and scrub its lavender timeline independently. The lavender ghost is the comparison clip at that exact time. With Onion Skin enabled, amber and cyan show its earlier and later samples. For combo joins, park the primary clip near its end and the comparison clip near its start. **Play Clip** drives the primary timeline with the comparison fixed at its cursor. Press **Space** to play or pause the primary clip, or **Left** and **Right** to pause and step to the previous or next key on the ruler. These shortcuts work from the Tweaker workspace when a field is not being edited. Drafts and **Set Key** apply to the primary clip.

In the Vermintide Viewer, open a compatible first-person model. Click a clip in the right sidebar to audition it, then Ctrl-click individual clips or Shift-click a range. Right-click a selected clip, choose the export for Tweaker action, and save the pack. In the Tweaker, open the player scene and click **Import Clips** in the Transform inspector. The dialog lists each clip with its detected weapon set. Choose **New library** and enter a name, or choose **Existing library** and select its destination. A batch appends clips in one editor undo action and skips source IDs already in that library. Save the player scene to keep the import.

Viewer packs evaluate the weapon sockets relative to the animated first-person camera. The import adds camera keys and native bone tracks when the preview rig has a matching hierarchy. Imported clips are available in **Clip** and **Compare**. A playable melee attack also needs its authored **HitStart** and **HitEnd** markers.

Open the player scene and click **Combo** in the Tweaker header. Add clips from the player's libraries to the timeline. Two clip rows show concurrent playback over each overlap. Drag a clip onto another to reorder it, or use the arrow buttons. Click a band in **Blends** to inspect the join at its midpoint. Choose a named Tweaker marker or **Custom Time** for the outgoing exit and incoming entry. To add a named point, return to the clip workspace, add a marker, and apply the clip draft.

The blend ends at the outgoing exit and begins at the incoming entry. Both clip clocks advance during the blend. **Loop Join** switches to a focused timeline and repeats the selected transition with a short lead-in and lead-out. You can change the blend while the join loops. **Sequence View** returns to the full timeline and pauses at the selected join. **Play Combo** runs the complete chain. **Close Loop** includes the last-to-first join and repeats playback. Press **Space** with the ruler focused to play or pause the current view. The ruler shares zoom, pan, and the playhead with the tracks. **Save** writes the ordered clip references and join settings as a separate resource, and **Open** reloads them. Combo settings drive the Tweaker preview.

The saved reference combo follows Kerillian's three light attacks, with a wind-up before each strike. To reopen it, assign its accompanying library under the `warhammer_1h_sword` name in the player, then choose **Open** in the Combo workspace. Its six clips retain the original socket, camera, and joint tracks. [Saved combo](../data/melee/kerillian_1h_sword_light_combo.tres) · [Clip library](../data/melee/warhammer_1h_sword_combo_library.tres)

With the [standalone native Tweaker addon](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/README.md) installed, **Project → Tools → Weapon Animation (Native)** opens the C++ authoring window. Carnage's project settings point both Tweakers at the Playground and give the native Tweaker a separate preview stage. Both windows can open the scripted and native combo resources. The original Tweaker scripts also keep existing animation resource references loadable.

Use **Scene** to choose a project scene and **Play Scene** to run it from the tweaker. The Playground is the default. Click **Open in Window** beside **Play Scene** for a separate, resizable workspace. **Dock Back** or the window close button returns the same draft to the editor tab. **Play Clip** controls the private animation preview. The preview uses the Playground sky and grid materials on the ground and reference meshes. Exponential fog blends the ground into the horizon.

Choose **Loop Clip** to repeat the full preview or **Play Once** to hold the final pose when playback ends. For a shorter loop, choose **Loop Region**, scrub to each boundary, and click **Set In** or **Set Out**. The In and Out fields accept precise times in seconds. The ruler highlights the active region, and playback begun outside it starts at In.

Drag the divider to resize the settings sidebar. Drag the horizontal divider under the viewport to resize the timeline, and the one below **Transform** to resize **Details**. Click **Target**, **Position**, **Rotation**, or **Clip Motion** to fold each Transform section. Under **Sword**, choose a model and adjust its mount in socket coordinates. Use **Move** to drag a parent-space axis or **Rotate** to drag a ring on the selected node's local axes. The numeric fields and sliders follow the gizmo. Use the crosshair to check where the sword sits relative to the camera.

To key the grip itself, choose **Target → Socket Keyframe**, select the socket, and scrub to the desired time. Adjust its position or rotation with the gizmo or numeric fields, then click **Set Key**. Set Key records that pose in the draft, updates the path and timeline, and resets the offset fields for the next edit. Repeat at other times, then use **Apply** or **Save As**. Keep the preview on the current frame until you have recorded its pose.

To edit source joints, instance the [optional pose hierarchy](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/native_pose/native_pose_rig.tscn) under the first-person camera before importing a clip with joint data. Press **Tab** to switch to **Pose** and inspect the skeleton. Both modes share the Playground sky, grid, and landmarks. The imported rig supplies the bones without the Warhammer arm mesh. The sockets and trails remain optional guides. Hold the right mouse button and use **WASD** and **Q/E** to fly. Middle drag orbits, Shift + middle drag pans, and the wheel zooms. Click a joint or bone segment to select it and use **F** to frame it. Use the Move or Rotate gizmo in Pose, then use **Set Key** to record the joint at the current time. Press **Tab** again to return to the first-person camera with the same joint selected. Saved variants retain the rig source. Trails follow the weapon sockets. The pose rig excludes hand and finger joints.

Imported camera keys animate the Look component's camera-local attack offset. Look combines it with mouse look, stair smoothing, and lateral roll. The Tweaker previews the same track. The `j_aim` joint belongs to the pose hierarchy.

Use the preview toolbar's **Undo** and **Redo**, or **Ctrl+Z** and **Ctrl+Shift+Z**, to step through draft edits and recorded keys. Use **Apply** to update the current clip and sword mount. **Save As** gives the motion draft a new name in the same library and selects that copy. Sword mount offsets stay in the draft until you apply them to the scene. Both actions support **Undo**. Save the player scene to use the edits in the Playground. [Editor plugin](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/plugin.gd)

Use **+ Track** in the Tweaker timeline to add a **Notify Track** or **Float Curve**. Right-click a notify lane to add a one-shot, a generic state, or a typed **MeleeTrace** state. Drag a state by its body to move it or by an edge to adjust its duration. Select a notify or key to edit its properties in **Details**. Names, colors, and numeric edits commit from their fields. Press **Delete** with a notify or key selected in the timeline to remove it. Right-click a track name and choose **Remove Track** to delete the row.

Click **+ Marker** or press **M** to drop a named Tweaker marker at the playhead. Give it a unique name in **Details**. The marker lane supports dragging and Delete, and duration adjustments scale marker times with the clip. Tweaker markers are separate clip data with their own names and colors. Game code can read them, curves, one-shot notifies, and notify states through `WeaponAnimationClip`. [Runtime API](weapon_animation_clip_api.md)

Float curves start as compact lanes tinted with their authored color. Use the arrow beside the name to open or close the graph in place. Right-click a float curve to add a key or choose **Frame Curve** to open the graph and fit its time and value range. **F** frames the selected curve while the graph has focus. Use the wheel to zoom or middle-drag to pan on both axes. Shift-click adds keys to the selection, Ctrl-click toggles keys, and dragging from empty graph space selects keys in a box. Drag any selected key to move the group. Choose **Time Only** or **Value Only** in the movement dropdown to lock an axis. In **Free** mode, hold Shift while dragging to lock to the dominant axis. Hold Ctrl while dragging to bypass time snapping.

Use **Key Mode** above the ruler, the **Interpolation** field in Details, or the right-click menu to set selected keys to **Constant**, **Linear**, **Auto**, or **Broken Tangents**. Right-click any selected key to edit the full selection. The graph distinguishes the modes by key shape and highlights selected keys. Constant holds the value until the next key. Auto computes cubic tangents from adjacent keys. Broken Tangents allows independent incoming and outgoing tangents. Enable **Weighted Tangents** in the right-click menu or **Weighted** in Details to edit the influence of a tangent with its handle length. The graph updates while dragging keys or handles. Select a curve or key to rename or recolor the curve in **Details**.

Drag **Blend Neighbor** left to move selected values toward the previous key, or right to move them toward the following key. Each contiguous group of selected keys uses its own neighbors. The graph previews the result during the drag, and releasing the slider commits one undoable edit at the original key times.

Choose **Save Curve as Preset** from a curve's context menu to store its name, color, and shape in the project. On another clip, pick that name under **+ Track → Curve Presets**. The added track has independent keys and retains the preset name. Its key times follow the new clip's duration. Track edits participate in draft Undo and Redo. Apply saves the tracks on the current clip, and Save As gives the new clip an independent copy. Save the player scene to persist either edit. [Curve preset catalog](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/curve_presets.gd)

Trimming either handle of a notify state keeps the playhead at the current preview time. State shading and the pose use that same playhead. Duration changes scale notify times and the curve's time axis with the motion. **MeleeTrace** is clip authoring data. The current melee controller still uses the clip's Godot `HitStart` and `HitEnd` markers for contact checks. [Clip track data](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/clip_tracks.gd) · [Melee timing](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

With the mouse captured, left click starts on the right hand and alternates on each accepted slash. The other hand's animation uses its idle pose. WeaponControl evaluates the animation during physics after Player movement and queries the attacking sword's sweep. The `HitStart` and `HitEnd` markers define the **0.20–0.35 s** contact window for both saved clips. The right slash ends at **0.70 s** and the left slash at **0.65 s**. One click during the slash queues the next hand and starts it at the end of recovery. **9** and **0** switch the right-hand sword between long and short, and the left hand holds a long sword. Switching the right sword or releasing mouse capture during a slash returns to idle and clears the queued input. [Playback](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd) · [Saved clips](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

**WeaponControl → Weapon Sway → Sway Settings** owns the response and four directional look poses. A damped spring tracks a target that accumulates applied mouse yaw and pitch and decays toward neutral. **Turn in Place** uses a second spring for long-sword yaw. A quick turn on the ground below **0.25 m/s** lengthens that spring's response, then lets the extra response decay without dropping the current pose. The short sword, pitch, and turns initiated while moving use the general response. The two hands mirror the sideways pose around their grips. The attacking sword eases out of sway during windup, follows its authored pose through the hit window, and regains sway during recovery. [Look input](../scripts/player/first_person_look.gd) · [Sway settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Sway playback](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

**Idle Breathing** advances a shared curve for both grips and samples it slightly later for blade pitch. It fades in as horizontal speed approaches rest on the floor, and fades out when movement, a jump, a dash, or a slash takes over. The curve keeps its phase across interruptions. `RESET` restores the carry at the end of a slash and the AnimationPlayer stops between swings. The breathing layer offsets the sword visuals at `MainGrip`. [Sway settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Saved curve](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

Under **Locomotion**, one damped spring uses actual horizontal acceleration after collision to lag the grips sideways and in depth. A second spring uses horizontal velocity to roll the blades into a strafe and pitch them into forward or backward travel. WeaponControl tracks both springs in world space, projects their outputs into the player frame, and applies the same attack fade as look sway. At steady speed the grips settle while the blades hold their slight lean. During braking below half **Full Speed**, the springs blend from **Response Time** toward **Settle Time** as speed falls. They carry their current offset and velocity into the return to idle. [Locomotion settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Movement](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd)

**Directional Carry** blends forward, backward, and leading or trailing strafe poses from smoothed, supported ground travel. Forward travel extends the grips slightly, backpedaling draws them in, and strafing gives the leading sword a different grip pose from the trailing sword. At a steady 45-degree diagonal, each fore-and-aft pose blends equally with its strafe pose. The carry fades to neutral at rest and applies at each `MainGrip` through the existing attack fade. [Pose settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Saved tuning](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

**Bob** advances one shared stride phase by actual horizontal travel after collision. The base step lengths are **0.92 m** at walking speed and **1.95 m** at full speed, blended by ground speed and adjusted by the style's steps-per-meter multiplier. Each step curve shapes one compression and recovery. The full stride alternates which side takes the load and drives opposing grip travel, with delayed wrist pitch. Strafing also biases the shared shift and roll into the direction of travel. [Saved curves](../scenes/player/player.tscn) · [Bob settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Bob playback](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

Movement measures travel on ticks supported by the floor or stairs. Vertical steps, airborne movement, dash steps, and passive platform carry do not advance the stride. The phase holds when travel stops while the grip offsets ease back in time. The bob uses the same grip pivot, attack fade, and wall clearance as the other weapon offsets. The opposing grip motion takes its cue from human arm swing, with position and rotation tuned for the sword stance. [Walking arm swing study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19640879/) · [Movement](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd)

**Bob → Style** selects a `WeaponBobStyle` resource. Its cadence, travel, tilt, and wrist delay layer over the base bob tuning. A style can supply its own walk and run curves. **Balanced** uses the saved base curves. **Light Footed** has faster, shallower steps, **Long Stride** has the slowest cadence, and **Heavy Braced** has a shallow, sustained foot plant with a wider side-to-side load transfer. Playground's **Alt+1–4** previews blend the style contributions at the shared stride phase and keep the selection local to the running player. [Style resources](../data/melee/bob_styles) · [Style fields](../scripts/melee/weapon_bob_style.gd) · [Preview controls](../scripts/levels/playground.gd)

**Heavy Braced** adds a mirrored lower, wider carry, yaw at full stride frequency, and a slower response to acceleration and braking. Its **Start Motion** plays on grounded movement onset, and **Pivot Motion** splits grip depth when actual travel changes direction. Both **Balanced** and **Heavy Braced** have a timed **Stop Motion** that starts as the player brakes or comes up against an obstacle. It retains the last travel direction and biases the shift toward the side carrying weight at braking onset. A direct reversal can use the same braking pose. These offsets blend with the selected style and use the same grip pivot, attack fade, and clearance check as sway. The gait comparison uses the player's configured walk speed and camera FOV. [Balanced style](../data/melee/bob_styles/balanced.tres) · [Heavy Braced style](../data/melee/bob_styles/heavy_braced.tres)

**Jump and Landing** in Weapon Sway Settings holds two authored reactions for every gait style. When Movement accepts a jump, WeaponControl plays the grip and wrist curves. The grip dips slightly first, with a later pitch and roll from the wrist curve, and both ease back toward the carry in the air. The landing curve blends from the current pose. Movement samples downward speed before `move_and_slide()` and reports it on a new floor contact. Landing strength eases from zero at **2 m/s** to full at **15 m/s** with the saved settings. Both effects use the existing attack fade and clearance path and apply only to the swords. [Movement events](../scripts/player/first_person_movement.gd) · [Weapon pose fields](../scripts/melee/weapon_air_motion.gd) · [Saved curves](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

Set the sword's **MainGrip** marker to the primary hand's hold point. The sway rotation uses that position as its pivot, with axes aligned to the camera. The long sword places **MainGrip** on the centerline near the guard and **SupportGrip** lower on the leather wrap. The short sword inherits the hilt and both markers. The visual clearance helper uses the weapon origin as the pivot for assets without **MainGrip**. [Sword scene](../scenes/weapons/prototype_sword.tscn)

Each sword owns a **MeleeSweep**. Move **SweepStart** and **SweepEnd** to set the base span. Rotate **SweepStart** around the blade axis to set the box orientation. **Width** and **Height** set the full cross-section in meters. [Sweep component](../scripts/melee/melee_sweep_3d.gd)

**WeaponControl → Slash Reach** holds the reach tuning for this attack. The same resource applies to either sword. The full length is `base span × 1.10 × Reach Multiplier + Flat Bonus`, clamped at zero. **Flat Bonus** adds meters to the length. **Blade Offset** moves both ends by the same distance along the blade, positive toward the tip and negative toward the grip. The saved values are **2×**, **+0.60 m**, and **+0.15 m**. The attack snapshots these settings when the slash starts. Clearing **Slash Reach** uses the weapon's base box. [Slash Reach](../data/melee/slash_reach.tres) · [Reach resource](../scripts/melee/melee_attack_reach.gd)

The Player scene previews the adjusted slash box as you scrub. A weapon scene opened on its own previews the base box. In play the sweep checks the opening overlap, then traces the previous blade span, blade rotation, and base translation between sampled poses. It reports one contact per physics body per swing and excludes the player.

**MeleeSweep → Debug** controls the editor preview, the in-game overlay, and the lifetime of the queried volumes. Enable **Show Sweeps** for the in-game overlay. Both saved swords have it disabled. Cyan shows the sweep geometry and orange marks contact with physics geometry. The Playground walls and blocks already have collision on the queried layer. [Reach comparison](../README.md#test-melee-reach)

**Wall Tuck** checks each sword separately from the camera through its swayed grip and the tip of its visual bounds. It measures the gap perpendicular to a steep surface on the clearance mask. The visual retracts along the line to the eye with projection-preserving scaling, so each grip retains its horizontal screen position. A small vertical trim keeps the guard in frame, and a contraction about `MainGrip` shortens the apparent blade slightly. Longer blades get extra notice distance from their visual reach. The tuck eases away as the path clears and uses the attack fade. [Tuck settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd)

**WeaponControl → Weapon Clearance** defaults to physics layer 1, **2.5 cm** of padding, and **0.12 s** for full recovery. After sway and tuck, WeaponControl checks each visible sword separately using a box enclosing the camera and the meshes under that sword's **Visuals** node. An overlap scales those meshes toward the camera while preserving their projected proportions. The grip markers and **MeleeSweep** stay on the authored pose. Put replacement meshes under **Visuals** to include their bounds. The saved sword meshes have shadow casting disabled. Lighting and reflections can still use the compressed world-space geometry. ## Blood impacts

The Playground places an animated, skinned test target and a backstop in front of the player. **MeleeSweep** reports a world-space point and normal when it finds a valid surface sample for a contacted body. **WeaponControl** samples each sword's **BloodMotion** marker between animation poses, falling back to **SweepEnd** and then to the sweep origin if the marker is absent. If the blade barely moves, the saved **Contact Direction Fallback** of zero resolves to camera forward. Starting with the contacted body and walking up its ancestors, **MeleeSweep** finds the nearest node that implements `receive_hit` and independently finds the nearest node with a direct **BloodComponent** child. It delivers the contact to both receivers when present, once each per swing. [Melee sweep](../scripts/melee/melee_sweep_3d.gd) · [Test target](../scenes/characters/blood_test_target.tscn)

Add **BloodComponent** directly under the scene root that owns the hit bodies, or under the hittable physics body itself. It handles the spray and world deposits with **Humanoid Blood** by default. Assign a **Blood Profile** on the component to override that preset. The profile groups the burst scene and its multipliers, world launch and projection settings, and body stain settings. Its **Splatter Style** references the shared artwork and appearance controls. Duplicate the profile for another enemy type and reuse the style or duplicate it for a different decal palette or mask set. The first hit creates a shared **BloodImpact** presenter for that level branch. The presenter starts the burst and owns **BloodWorld** as an internal child. Each queued world droplet keeps the profile until impact, so it can finish after the hit target despawns. World caps, lifetimes, and collision settings belong to the internal pool. [Component scene](../scenes/effects/blood_component.tscn) · [Coordinator](../scripts/effects/blood_impact.gd) · [Profile fields](../scripts/effects/blood_profile.gd)

On a plain collider the airborne burst starts 1.5 cm beneath the sampled contact. With a configured skinned mesh and a bone-attached hit body, the component probes the mesh's cached bind-pose triangles in the struck bone's frame. The burst starts 1.5 cm beneath the nearest rendered triangle along its normal, or uses an inward collider center if the surface probe misses. The body stain and world droplets use the original collision point. [Blood component](../scripts/effects/blood_component.gd)

The saved **BloodBurst** launches three one-shot GPU emitters in world space. **ImpactMist** builds a dense, short-lived, stippled cloud at the hit. **LiquidPatches** draws thin torn filaments and a smaller group of delayed cohesive lobes that lift and break into flecks. **Droplets** sends finer, irregular spray along independently damped trajectories. The procedural draw shader softens particles at opaque scene depth. The coordinator shuffles ten launch patterns before repeating. The scene and draw material supply the authored defaults. The profile multiplies tint, emitter counts, speed, spread, and liquid trail length per burst. [Burst scene](../particles/blood_burst.tscn) · [GPU motion shader](../shaders/effects/blood_burst_process.gdshader) · [Draw shader](../shaders/effects/blood_burst.gdshader)

**BloodWorld** launches an invisible physics droplet with a sweep radius of 1 cm. Its speed comes from the enemy profile. These world queries place decals independently of the visible burst. On character hits, **Character Spray Spread** varies the launch direction, and a second, steeper droplet deposits fallout near the target's feet. Both droplets use gravity and swept world queries, excluding the attacker and target. Fallout stops at obstructions and only marks floors. Wall projections start at 1.80 × 2.15 m and project through 22 cm. **Wall Center Shift** moves each wall stamp along the surface while keeping its collision point inside the projection. Floor projections start at 1.95 × 2.15 m with 16 cm of depth and stretch on glancing impacts. Fallout uses 76% of that footprint before style variation. World marks attach to the hit node when available and share a 60-second lifetime and 50-mark cap in the saved Playground. **Blood Profile → Debug → Show Droplet Wires** draws the active physics spheres for that profile at their query radius in play. Wires disappear when the corresponding droplets land or expire. [World settings](../scenes/effects/blood_world.tscn) · [Decal](../scenes/effects/blood_world_decal.tscn)

Rigid props get a shallow decal at the hit, attached to the mesh so it follows movement. The component uses an explicitly assigned **Body Stain Mesh → Surface**, the closest mesh on the hit body's path to the component's parent, or the sole visible rigid mesh under that parent. When the collision shape is slightly offset, the component checks for the visible surface near the contact and projects from that point. The decal uses the selected mesh's visual layers.

For skinned body stains, assign the component's **Body Stain Mesh → Skeleton** and **Surface**. Give surface 0 the stain shader and put the hit bodies directly under bone attachments on the assigned skeleton. The component converts the contact from the animated hit bone to the mesh's bind pose. The shader samples bind position and normal from `CUSTOM0` and `CUSTOM1`, so the projected textures follow the skinned surface. Both types retain up to **Stain Limit** marks per target. At the limit, a new hit replaces the oldest mark. **Stain Size** sets the footprint before variation, and **Stain Depth** defaults to 8 cm on either side of the contact plane. The mannequin renders on visual layer 2, while the world decal's render cull mask projects onto visual layer 1. The droplet separately queries physics layer 1 through **World Mask**. [Mannequin setup](../scenes/characters/blood_test_target.tscn) · [Stain shader](../shaders/effects/blood_stain.gdshader)

The shared **Blood Splatter Style** owns tint, footprint, angle variation, and wet finish. Floor and wall hits use the same sixteen generated deposits from **Floor Variants**. Each includes an albedo and coverage map and a nonmetallic roughness map. Pooled deposits also use a shallow relief normal. **Wall Drips** variants follow the surface's downhill direction with a small angle variation. The wall shuffles a pool per profile and uses every available texture before repeating, including a check against immediate repeats when reshuffling. Dense patches are smoother than their edges. **Floor Albedo Mix** controls how much receiver color remains under the deposit. Body hits alternate two generated cast-off patterns with matched roughness. **Wet Roughness** is the fallback for a pattern without a roughness texture. Oblique hits alternate rotation sides around the projected motion. Floor and wall rotation histories are tracked separately. Nearly head-on hits turn 60–150° from the previous offset. [Style settings](../data/effects/blood_splatter_style.tres) · [Godot decal material channels](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/latest/classes/class_decal.html)

The deposit textures use 640 × 640 albedo and coverage maps with 384 × 384 roughness and relief maps. Their silhouettes came from three generated grayscale atlases. The supplied *Vermintide 2* footage and read-only study of its installed textures and VFX code informed the shape, buildup, and timing. [Deposit textures](../textures/effects/blood_deposits)

The player's **LensBlood** scene draws temporary drops and edge spray in the shared **ScreenEffects** canvas. It uses four regions from the drop atlas, imported as white with the source alpha, and four grayscale spray regions. **Drop Material → Shader Parameters → Drop Color** tints the small drops through their alpha masks. The spray material samples a softened mip level of the atlas and feathers its luminance into alpha coverage. **Edge Tint** supplies the spray color and limits its opacity. Each new mark picks a variant different from the previous mark of its kind. Large spray marks use square quads with side lengths of **0.30–0.60 times half the viewport diagonal**. Their centers are sampled on a ring **0.40–0.50** from screen center in normalized screen coordinates. Small drops slide down at an editable fraction of the viewport height per second, and the spray stays in place as it fades. The scene also owns burst timings, lifetimes, opacity, and the active mark cap. [Lens overlay](../scenes/effects/lens_blood.tscn) · [Spray material shader](../shaders/effects/lens_blood_spray.gdshader) · [Drop artwork](../textures/effects/lens_blood_atlas.png) · [Edge spray atlas](../textures/effects/lens_blood_spray_atlas.png)

The saved **LensBlood** scene emits six small drops per burst at **0, 0.05, and 0.15 seconds** after a splatter starts. Drops vary in height, aspect ratio, and opacity. **Drop Material → Shader Parameters → Softness** controls how much the mipmapped drop mask is blurred. The Playground wires **WeaponControl** contact and swing signals to a lens preview that plays once per swing for contacts on the mannequin within **3 m** of the camera, measured to the mannequin's root plus one meter of height. This preview uses a contact because the mannequin has no death state. [Playground trigger](../scripts/levels/playground.gd)

## Damage vignette

**DamageVignette** draws before **LensBlood** in the shared screen canvas. Its shader samples the alpha of an original **512 × 512** feathered mask to multiply the scene toward a dark red at the edges. **Mask Size** sets the width and height of the centered texture in units of half the viewport diagonal. **WeaponControl** relays overlaps from the active sword's sweep. The Playground pulses the vignette on the first overlap per swing, including wall hits, so a swing that touches several bodies produces one pulse. The standalone effect uses a **15 ms** rise and **185 ms** linear fade based on *Vermintide 2*'s incoming-hit particle. The saved Player instance uses a **650 ms** fade. A new hit restarts the pulse from its current strength. ## Damage direction HUD

**DamageIndicator** owns the authored style, layout, tint, body, and glow settings in its Inspector. The scene offers **Worn A1**, **Original A1**, **Classic**, **Ash Arc**, **Split Brush**, and **Torn Chevron**. The temporary editor preview draws one to eight markers in the active scene, adding them clockwise from the top at **45°** intervals. It uses the same tinted alpha mask and native bloom passes as a runtime hit. The translucent body draws into the HUD. **BloomViewport** draws emission over black in HDR 2D and runs Godot's **Canvas** environment glow in **Replace** mode. **BloomComposite** adds the resulting RGB over the main viewport, where the Playground uses **Sky**. The emission viewport resizes with the HUD and goes idle when there are no runtime markers or when the editor preview is off or leaves the active scene. The editor saves the scene with the bloom viewport idle.

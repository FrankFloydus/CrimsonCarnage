## Dynamic Sky addon

West Ridge Observatory instances the Dynamic Sky rig. The rig resolves global and local weather, drives the atmosphere and celestial lights, and forwards conditions to precipitation, spatial audio, and weather-responsive surfaces. The Volumetric renderer and the native directional light projector share the cloud field, with cheaper projection paths for 2D Dynamic and Static. A distant lightning controller reports the strike position for the localized sky flash and travel-time-delayed thunder. The Playground uses the Storm preset, 2D Dynamic clouds, and its own WorldEnvironment resource. The UDW comparison scene instances the same Dynamic Sky rig with a reflected reference camera, test geometry, and weather-responsive checker materials. Its capture controls belong to the comparison scene root. [UDW comparison](../addons/dynamic_sky/demo/udw_comparison/README.md) · [Authoring and runtime API](dynamic_sky.md) · [Unreal reference map](ultra_dynamic_sky_audit.md)

Radial Storm actors participate in the same-world weather sampler and own their horizontal footprint and travel. Their precipitation easing uses the weather resolved so far and the storm's target. Material receivers use separate linear accumulation inputs while the rain impact channel follows the resolved rain. In Volumetric mode, the rig sends the ordered storm footprints in rig-relative kilometers to the shared cloud field. The visible sky, native shadow projector, and in-cloud fog evaluate the same coverage. The actor owns its depth-tested exterior precipitation shaft, obscured flash, and distant thunder bed. These components resolve weather and listener state from the Dynamic Sky rig in their World3D. The optional root scheduler selects a seasonal profile and starts the same saved actor and lifecycle used for manual authoring. [Radial Storm authoring](dynamic_sky.md#radial-storm) · [Weather sampler](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/weather_state.gd) · [Scheduler](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/radial_storm_scheduler.gd)

The main DynamicSky script owns effect authoring through typed resources for rain, snow, airborne haze, screen weather, optional camera shake and debris, audio, lightning, rainbow optics, and cloud shadows. It applies them before internal child initialization and on resource changes. Optional Configuration groups the same settings. The root owns the camera and listener references. Internal components retain standalone fallbacks. WorldEnvironment remains the sole native exposed child for rendering resources. [Root effect authoring](dynamic_sky.md#author-effects-on-the-main-rig)

Weather profiles can opt into a daylight atmosphere contribution and density-limited material emission for the visible volumetric cloud pass. The rig derives the weight and atmosphere radiance from the current profile and Sun elevation. The cubemap keeps its separate lighting pass. [Profile control](../addons/dynamic_sky/dynamic_sky_weather.gd) · [Sky shader](../addons/dynamic_sky/rendering/dynamic_sky.gdshader)

**Weather Style** on Dynamic Sky selects the active profile family. Stylized profiles pair the shared weather channels with editable sky, fog, light, and particle settings. The rig dispatches rain and snow visuals to the corresponding emitter scene. Weather transitions, zones, climate, and snapshot restores operate within the selected family. [Stylized weather authoring](dynamic_sky.md#stylized-weather)

The rig's Exposure Settings resource owns observer attenuation above volumetric clouds and beneath authored water surfaces, along with fallback exposure in heavy dust and opt-in nighttime Storm calibration. The saved WeatherExposure child filters the camera's resolved local weather before the sky and camera effects consume it. Global weather, per-position physical wind, cloud motion, and material accumulation retain their unfiltered state. Saved Box or Sphere water regions define horizontal footprints and surface heights. The demo lake owns one such region. Nighttime Storm can scale global lunar and cubemap illumination while the observer's local weather controls the optional camera fallback and shadow grade. Authored camera attributes and Environment adjustments keep precedence. [Exposure authoring](dynamic_sky.md#local-weather-and-materials) · [Water Region scene](../addons/dynamic_sky/weather/exposure/water_region.tscn)

The demo's terrain mesh owns meadow, trail, drainage, and forest masks in its vertex colors. Saved materials own PBR maps, physical texture scales, slope and altitude transitions, and coating appearance. A shared triplanar sampler keeps stochastic albedo, normal, and ORM transforms aligned across terrain and optional world-projected weather materials. `DynamicSkySurface` owns accumulation for explicit surface components. The root's internal material binding owns it for custom shaders with the shared weather hook, including the prototype grid. Both use the same accumulation calculation. For compatible meshes, the root overlays up to eight prioritized radial coating targets at fragment positions over the accumulated baseline. A private texture carries the field for its World3D. Rain contacts, radial coatings, and snow tracks share one runtime material copy when they use the same slot and release their own parameters independently. The fine and geological PBR bake tools derive shading maps from the original ImageGen artwork. [Terrain and surface authoring](dynamic_sky.md#local-weather-and-materials) · [Radial material field](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/radial_material_field.gd) · [Artwork provenance](dynamic_sky_imagegen.md)

Rain can render the existing falling-drop contact particles to a private viewport for nearby opaque material normals. The root's material binding supplies mesh candidates in the same World3D. The field binds the texture to compatible runtime material slots. The last effect to release a slot restores its authored assignment. [Rain authoring](dynamic_sky.md#author-effects-on-the-main-rig) · [Field controller](../addons/dynamic_sky/precipitation/rain/contact_normals/rain_contact_normal_field.gd)

An opt-in Snow Track Receiver uses the accumulated snow on its mesh and a bounded local field to displace visible vertices. A Snow Track Stamp submits accepted footfall or swept-contact marks to that receiver. The base collision continues to handle grounding. [Deep snow track authoring](dynamic_sky_snow_tracks.md)

## Movement and jumping

The player boots the stock Framework controller from **Controls**. Its native Mover driver advances the body once per physics tick from **Mover → Ground Movement**, **Air Movement**, and **Jumping**. **FPS / Shoulder Aim** with **Character Heading** lets the game’s Look script own aim and yaw. The Input Layout enables click recapture and assigns the Escape release action in its always-active Gameplay map. The player enables its Locomotion input map while Controls owns the captured mouse. Releasing the mouse clears steering and leaves native braking, gravity, and collision active. [Player scene](../scenes/player/player.tscn) · [Controller](../scripts/player/player.gd) · [Input layout](../data/player/default_input_layout.tres)

Grounded characters automatically climb walkable stair risers that fit their capsule. **Mover → Stairs → Max Step Height** defaults to **0.30 m**. The same limit extends downward floor snap when following stairs. At zero, the body uses its normal floor snap. The saved player's jump speed is **6 m/s** and gravity scale is **1.5**. [Player scene](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

## Crouch and camera

Assign the same [Crouch Action resource](../data/player/crouch_action.tres) in the Locomotion map and under **Controls → Player Setup → Crouch Action**, then enable the stance under **Mover → Crouch**. The resource has a **Pressed** trigger. The native player controller routes that trigger and the button’s held state from Controls to Mover, which resolves crouch before the movement step. Crouching changes the capsule to **1.10 m** while keeping its bottom at the feet, and the eye blends to **0.95 m** over **0.20 s**. Standing checks the full capsule. **Toggle** requires a fresh press after a blocked stand. **One Shot** retries after release when clearance returns. [Crouch settings](../data/player/default_crouch_settings.tres)

The stock driver samples **Controls → Player Setup → Mouse Look Action** from the Locomotion map, scales it by the configured mouse sensitivity, and delivers the signed aim delta to Look once per physics tick. Look applies body yaw and clamps eye pitch using **Camera3D → Aim → Pitch Limit**, then reports the applied turn to weapon sway. The same script composes the **Camera3D → Strafe Roll** effect with the imported `Look:attack_camera_motion` track. The native MoverComponent handles the committed stair height, the smoothing spring, and camera clearance. **Mover → Stairs → Stair Smoothing Time** controls the blend. Look applies the resulting camera-local offset when composing the view. [Look controller](../scripts/player/first_person_look.gd)

Weapon presentation runs after stock movement and plays its takeoff curve when the body leaves the floor moving upward. Live Mover publishes `landed(impact_speed)` after an established movement attachment lands from a descent. `get_last_fall_distance()` exposes the drop to touchdown from the highest airborne or departure sample. Player connects both listeners at Controls boot. WeaponControl uses impact speed for its pose in sword form, and Player passes fall distance to Stats in either form. Initial floor acquisition after spawn or teleport and continuous stair support are filtered by Mover. [Player wiring](../scripts/player/player.gd) · [Weapon presentation](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

**Camera3D → Movement FOV** layers an offset over the saved **55°** FOV. The player scene sets full-speed offsets of **+1.5°** forward and **−0.1°** backward, with a **0.24 s** response time. Look scales the offset by actual speed projected along the body's forward axis. Pure strafing contributes zero, and the camera eases back to its base FOV at rest. Camera3D can compose this with the separate dash FOV channel through `set_dash_active()`. Set either movement offset to zero to disable that direction. [Camera settings](../scripts/player/first_person_camera_effects.gd) · [Saved camera](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

## Post-processing

**ScreenCopy** captures the scene after the dash and lens effects have drawn. **PostProcessing → Pass** uses that copy for a painterly filter, the ordinary vignette, a screen-aligned **4 × 4 Bayer** pattern, and per-channel posterization, in that order. Applying the dither before quantization breaks up color-band boundaries. **DamageVignette** draws after that pass in **PlayerPresentation → ScreenEffects** on canvas layer **0**. The HUD at layer **2** renders above the stack and includes its HDR bloom composite. [PostProcessing scene](../scenes/effects/post_processing.tscn) · [Shader](../shaders/effects/post_processing.gdshader) · [Player presentation](../scenes/player/player_presentation.tscn)

The painterly filter favors flatter neighborhoods close to the source color and groups their tones with a soft luminance curve. **Brush Stroke Strength** controls the contrast of angled paint marks. **Edge Sharpness** restores more of the source at local color boundaries. The external **Post Processing** material owns the stack settings and receives live changes through Godot's **Synchronize Scene Changes**. In the Playground, **P** calls `PlayerPresentation.toggle_painterly_preview()` to bypass the painterly filter on its own canvas item. The Playground also routes the mannequin's lens splatter and the directional HUD preview. Stats owns the event that drives the incoming damage vignette. [Material](../materials/effects/post_processing.tres) · [Playground preview](../scripts/levels/playground.gd) · [Presentation wiring](../scripts/player/player_presentation.gd)

The **Post Processing** editor plugin draws the saved pass in the 3D editor viewports using the active scene's material. Its preview layers are editor-only and follow the active scene. The game pass runs in the canvas after tonemapping and the gameplay overlays. Godot's Compositor callbacks run during 3D rendering, before that canvas stage. [Preview plugin](../addons/post_processing_preview/plugin.gd) · [Post-processing controls](../README.md#tune-post-processing) · [Godot Compositor](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html)

## Combat form mockup

**Stats** attaches the Framework's local `vitals` attribute set. The player starts at **100/100 health** and **0/300 blood**. Stats clamps current values to their respective maxima and caps maximum blood at **300**. PlayerPresentation subscribes to Stats and updates both HUD bars. [Vitals definition](../project.godot) · [Stats](../scripts/player/player_stats.gd)

Damage sources call `PlayerStats.apply_damage(amount)`. It clamps health to zero, returns the actual loss, and emits `damage_taken(actual_loss)` when health drops. PlayerPresentation pulses the incoming damage vignette for that event. Player sends Mover's landing distance to `apply_fall_damage()`. In the saved scene the first **4 m** are safe and each metre beyond that costs **5 health**. The Stats script supplies defaults of **3 m** and **15 health per metre** to instances without overrides. Tune both values under **Stats → Fall Damage**. [Landing wiring](../scripts/player/player.gd) · [Damage rules](../scripts/player/player_stats.gd) · [Saved tuning](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

The **Melee Slash** action goes to one WeaponControl. A started swing grants **50 blood** whether it hits or misses. The saved player uses a swing-start stand-in while the sweep and attack slots are empty. At **300 blood**, **Activate Spellcaster** changes form and hides the sword mounts. Blood drains on the physics clock at **30 points per second** by default. Edit the `vitals.blood_drain` stat under **Stats → Blood Drain**. At zero, Player restores sword visibility, input, and carry. The current form workflow operates on the mounted scenes directly. ### Native animation authoring

The independent **Weapon Animation (Native)** GDExtension owns its C++ window and clip, track, and combo resource classes. The editor launches from **Project → Tools → Weapon Animation (Native)**. Select the **AnimationPlayer** in the player scene and use **Refresh Rig** if needed. The project points **Play Scene** at the Playground and gives the window an authored preview stage. The private preview samples a copy of the camera rig and evaluates pose tracks without gameplay callbacks. [Native addon](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/README.md) · [Window and controls](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/src/modules/weapon_animation/editor/README.md) · [Preview stage](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/preview_reference.tscn)

In the Vermintide Viewer, audition clips from the right sidebar. Ctrl-click individual clips or Shift-click a range, then right-click the selection and export a Tweaker pack. Use **Import Clips** in the native window, choose the pack and destination library, and save the player scene. Source IDs already present in the library are skipped. The importer maps each clip's camera-relative weapon sockets and supported camera and joint tracks to the edited player rig. Instance the optional skeleton under the first-person camera before importing joint data. [Import workflow](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/src/modules/weapon_animation/editor/README.md#import-viewer-animations) · [Optional source skeleton](../addons/weapon_animation_tweaker/native_pose/native_pose_rig.tscn)

Use **Transform** to edit a socket, mount, or joint. **Set Key** records its current pose, and **Set Mount** records a model transform. **Sockets** shows the grip guides and **Trails** shows the socket paths with keyframe dots. **Tab** switches between the first-person and pose views. The optional rig carries source joints without arm meshes or fingers. Imported `Look:attack_camera_motion` keys feed the same camera composition used in play. The `j_aim` joint is part of the pose hierarchy. [Clip authoring controls](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/src/modules/weapon_animation/editor/README.md#edit-a-clip-draft) · [Look script](../scripts/player/first_person_look.gd)

Scrub the timeline with key snapping or hold **Ctrl** for free scrubbing. Zoom with the wheel, middle-drag to pan, and use **Fit** to see the whole clip. With timeline focus, **Space** plays or pauses and the arrows step through authored keys. Choose **Play Once**, **Loop Clip**, or **Loop Region**. Set In and Out at the playhead for a shorter loop. The comparison picker adds a separate clip and playhead. **Onion** sets earlier and later pose counts and sample spacing. Zero spacing uses the clip step. **Apply** records the draft in editor history, and **Save Variant** creates an independent clip in the current library. Save the scene to persist the edit. [Window and controls](../native/weapon_animation_tweaker/src/modules/weapon_animation/editor/README.md)

Godot's built-in animation markers are stored separately. ### Procedural weapon motion

**WeaponControl → Weapon Sway → Sway Settings** owns the response and four directional look poses. A damped spring tracks a target that accumulates applied mouse yaw and pitch and decays toward neutral. **Turn in Place** uses a second spring for long-sword yaw. A quick turn on the ground below **0.25 m/s** lengthens that spring's response, then lets the extra response decay without dropping the current pose. The short sword, pitch, and turns initiated while moving use the general response. The two hands mirror the sideways pose around their grips. The attacking sword eases out of sway during windup, follows its authored pose through the hit window, and regains sway during recovery. [Look input](../scripts/player/first_person_look.gd) · [Sway settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Sway playback](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

**Idle Breathing** advances a shared grip curve and samples it slightly later for blade pitch. It fades in as horizontal speed approaches rest on the floor and out during movement, a jump, or a slash. The curve keeps its phase across interruptions. [Sway settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Saved curve](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

Under **Locomotion**, one damped spring uses actual horizontal acceleration after collision to lag the grips sideways and in depth. A second spring uses horizontal velocity to roll the blades into a strafe and pitch them into forward or backward travel. WeaponControl tracks both springs in world space, projects their outputs into the player frame, and applies the same attack fade as look sway. At steady speed the grips settle while the blades hold their slight lean. During braking below half **Full Speed**, the springs blend from **Response Time** toward **Settle Time** as speed falls. They carry their current offset and velocity into the return to idle. [Locomotion settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Mover component](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

**Directional Carry** blends forward, backward, and leading or trailing strafe poses from smoothed, supported ground travel. Forward travel extends the grips slightly, backpedaling draws them in, and strafing gives the leading sword a different grip pose from the trailing sword. At a steady 45-degree diagonal, each fore-and-aft pose blends equally with its strafe pose. The carry fades to neutral at rest and applies at each `MainGrip` through the existing attack fade. [Pose settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Saved tuning](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

**Bob** advances one shared stride phase by actual horizontal travel after collision. The base step lengths are **0.92 m** at walking speed and **1.95 m** at full speed, blended by ground speed and adjusted by the style's steps-per-meter multiplier. Each step curve shapes one compression and recovery. The full stride alternates which side takes the load and drives opposing grip travel, with delayed wrist pitch. Strafing also biases the shared shift and roll into the direction of travel. [Saved curves](../scenes/player/player.tscn) · [Bob settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd) · [Bob playback](../scripts/melee/first_person_melee.gd)

The weapon rig reads the body's horizontal travel on ticks supported by the floor. The phase holds when travel stops while the grip offsets ease back in time. The bob uses the same grip pivot, attack fade, and wall clearance as the other weapon offsets. The opposing grip motion takes its cue from human arm swing, with position and rotation tuned for the sword stance. [Walking arm swing study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19640879/) · [Mover component](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

**Bob → Style** selects a `WeaponBobStyle` resource. Its cadence, travel, tilt, and wrist delay layer over the base bob tuning. A style can supply its own walk and run curves. **Balanced** uses the saved base curves. **Light Footed** has faster, shallower steps, **Long Stride** has the slowest cadence, and **Heavy Braced** has a shallow, sustained foot plant with a wider side-to-side load transfer. Playground's **Alt+1–4** previews blend the style contributions at the shared stride phase and keep the selection local to the running player. [Style resources](../data/melee/bob_styles) · [Style fields](../scripts/melee/weapon_bob_style.gd) · [Preview controls](../scripts/levels/playground.gd)

**Heavy Braced**, the saved active style, adds a lower, wider carry and a slower locomotion response. **Start Motion** plays on grounded movement onset. **Pivot Motion** splits grip depth on a change of travel direction. The saved **Braced Stop** plays for **0.55 s**, with **9 mm** fore-and-aft travel, **0.65°** of pitch, and up to **3 mm** of sideways weight shift. The current adapter triggers it when travel drops below the gait threshold, and the rig can also use the stop pose on a near-opposite reversal. [Balanced style](../data/melee/bob_styles/balanced.tres) · [Heavy Braced style](../data/melee/bob_styles/heavy_braced.tres)

**Jump and Landing** applies camera-local grip translation and blade rotation from signed curves. Curve time runs from zero to the authored Duration. Each curve value scales its associated offset. Rotation uses its own curve when assigned. The saved takeoff and landing motions both last **0.40 s** and use vertical dip and pitch. Landing strength blends from the **2 m/s** threshold to full strength at the saved **10 m/s**. A jump during landing recovery crossfades from the current pose. [Air motion settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_air_motion.gd) · [Saved curves and values](../scenes/player/player.tscn)

Set the sword's **MainGrip** marker to the primary hand's hold point. The sway rotation uses that position as its pivot, with axes aligned to the camera. The prototype sword places **MainGrip** at the model origin inside the leather wrap. The helper uses the socket origin when it cannot resolve a single mount's **MainGrip**. ### Sweep reach and clearance

For a weapon with gameplay tracing, add a **MeleeSweep** and place **SweepStart** and **SweepEnd** along the blade. Rotate **SweepStart** around the blade axis to orient the contact box. **Width** and **Height** set its full cross-section in metres. Assign the sweep to the matching WeaponControl slot. [Sweep component](../scripts/melee/melee_sweep_3d.gd)

**WeaponControl → Slash Reach** holds the reach tuning for this attack. The same resource applies to either sword. The full length is `base span × 1.10 × Reach Multiplier + Flat Bonus`, clamped at zero. **Flat Bonus** adds meters to the length. **Blade Offset** moves both ends by the same distance along the blade, positive toward the tip and negative toward the grip. The saved values are **2×**, **+0.60 m**, and **+0.15 m**. The attack snapshots these settings when the slash starts. Clearing **Slash Reach** uses the weapon's base box. [Slash Reach](../data/melee/slash_reach.tres) · [Reach resource](../scripts/melee/melee_attack_reach.gd)

For an assigned sweep, the player scene previews the adjusted slash box as the animation is scrubbed. The weapon scene on its own previews the base box. In play the sweep checks the opening overlap, then traces the span, rotation, and translation between sampled poses. It reports one contact per physics body per swing and excludes the player.

**MeleeSweep → Debug** controls the editor preview, the in-game overlay, and the lifetime of the queried volumes. Enable **Show Sweeps** for the in-game overlay. Cyan shows the sweep geometry and orange marks contact with physics geometry. The Playground walls and blocks already have collision on the queried layer. [Weapon setup](../README.md#preview-and-author-weapons)

**Wall Tuck** checks each sword separately from the camera through its swayed grip and the tip of its visual bounds. It measures the gap perpendicular to a steep surface on the clearance mask. The visual retracts along the line to the eye with projection-preserving scaling, so each grip retains its horizontal screen position. A small vertical trim keeps the guard in frame, and a contraction about `MainGrip` shortens the apparent blade slightly. Longer blades get extra notice distance from their visual reach. The tuck eases away as the path clears and uses the attack fade. [Tuck settings](../scripts/melee/weapon_sway_settings.gd)

**WeaponControl → Weapon Clearance** defaults to physics layer 1, **2.5 cm** of padding, and **0.12 s** for full recovery. After sway and tuck, WeaponControl checks each visible sword separately using a box enclosing the camera and the visible meshes on that hand's socket. An overlap scales those meshes toward the camera while preserving their projected proportions. The grip markers and **MeleeSweep** stay on the authored pose. Mount a mesh or a weapon scene under either socket to include its visible geometry. The rig applies sway and clearance to the rendered meshes, leaving socket and sweep transforms available for animation and tracing. The saved sword meshes have shadow casting disabled. Lighting and reflections can still use the compressed world-space geometry. ## Blood impacts

The Playground places an animated, skinned test target and a backstop in front of the player. With an attack and sweep assigned, **MeleeSweep** reports a world-space point and normal when it finds a valid surface sample for a contacted body. **WeaponControl** samples each sword's **BloodMotion** marker between animation poses, falling back to **SweepEnd** and then to the sweep origin if the marker is absent. If the blade barely moves, the saved **Contact Direction Fallback** of zero resolves to camera forward. Starting with the contacted body and walking up its ancestors, **MeleeSweep** finds the nearest node that implements `receive_hit` and independently finds the nearest node with a direct **BloodComponent** child. It delivers the contact to both receivers when present, once each per swing. [Melee sweep](../scripts/melee/melee_sweep_3d.gd) · [Test target](../scenes/characters/blood_test_target.tscn)

Add **BloodComponent** directly under the scene root that owns the hit bodies, or under the hittable physics body itself. It handles the spray and world deposits with **Humanoid Blood** by default. Assign a **Blood Profile** on the component to override that preset. The profile groups the burst scene and its multipliers, world launch and projection settings, and body stain settings. Its **Splatter Style** references the shared artwork and appearance controls. Duplicate the profile for another enemy type and reuse the style or duplicate it for a different decal palette or mask set. The first hit creates a shared **BloodImpact** presenter for that level branch. The presenter starts the burst and owns **BloodWorld** as an internal child. Each queued world droplet keeps the profile until impact, so it can finish after the hit target despawns. World caps, lifetimes, and collision settings belong to the internal pool. [Component scene](../scenes/effects/blood_component.tscn) · [Coordinator](../scripts/effects/blood_impact.gd) · [Profile fields](../scripts/effects/blood_profile.gd)

On a plain collider the airborne burst starts 1.5 cm beneath the sampled contact. With a configured skinned mesh and a bone-attached hit body, the component probes the mesh's cached bind-pose triangles in the struck bone's frame. The burst starts 1.5 cm beneath the nearest rendered triangle along its normal, or uses an inward collider center if the surface probe misses. The body stain and world droplets use the original collision point. [Blood component](../scripts/effects/blood_component.gd)

The saved **BloodBurst** launches three one-shot GPU emitters in world space. **ImpactMist** builds a dense, short-lived, stippled cloud at the hit. **LiquidPatches** draws thin torn filaments and a smaller group of delayed cohesive lobes that lift and break into flecks. **Droplets** sends finer, irregular spray along independently damped trajectories. The procedural draw shader softens particles at opaque scene depth. The coordinator shuffles ten launch patterns before repeating. The scene and draw material supply the authored defaults. The profile multiplies tint, emitter counts, speed, spread, and liquid trail length per burst. [Burst scene](../particles/blood_burst.tscn) · [GPU motion shader](../shaders/effects/blood_burst_process.gdshader) · [Draw shader](../shaders/effects/blood_burst.gdshader)

**BloodWorld** launches an invisible physics droplet with a sweep radius of 1 cm. Its speed comes from the enemy profile. These world queries place decals independently of the visible burst. On character hits, **Character Spray Spread** varies the launch direction, and a second, steeper droplet deposits fallout near the target's feet. Both droplets use gravity and swept world queries, excluding the attacker and target. Fallout stops at obstructions and only marks floors. Wall projections start at 1.80 × 2.15 m and project through 22 cm. **Wall Center Shift** moves each wall stamp along the surface while keeping its collision point inside the projection. Floor projections start at 1.95 × 2.15 m with 16 cm of depth and stretch on glancing impacts. Fallout uses 76% of that footprint before style variation. World marks attach to the hit node when available and share a 60-second lifetime and 50-mark cap in the saved Playground. **Blood Profile → Debug → Show Droplet Wires** draws the active physics spheres for that profile at their query radius in play. Wires disappear when the corresponding droplets land or expire. [World settings](../scenes/effects/blood_world.tscn) · [Decal](../scenes/effects/blood_world_decal.tscn)

Rigid props get a shallow decal at the hit, attached to the mesh so it follows movement. The component uses an explicitly assigned **Body Stain Mesh → Surface**, the closest mesh on the hit body's path to the component's parent, or the sole visible rigid mesh under that parent. When the collision shape is slightly offset, the component checks for the visible surface near the contact and projects from that point. The decal uses the selected mesh's visual layers.

For skinned body stains, assign the component's **Body Stain Mesh → Skeleton** and **Surface**. Give surface 0 the stain shader and put the hit bodies directly under bone attachments on the assigned skeleton. The component converts the contact from the animated hit bone to the mesh's bind pose. The shader samples bind position and normal from `CUSTOM0` and `CUSTOM1`, so the projected textures follow the skinned surface. Both types retain up to **Stain Limit** marks per target. At the limit, a new hit replaces the oldest mark. **Stain Size** sets the footprint before variation, and **Stain Depth** defaults to 8 cm on either side of the contact plane. The mannequin renders on visual layer 2, while the world decal's render cull mask projects onto visual layer 1. The droplet separately queries physics layer 1 through **World Mask**. [Mannequin setup](../scenes/characters/blood_test_target.tscn) · [Stain shader](../shaders/effects/blood_stain.gdshader)

The shared **Blood Splatter Style** owns tint, footprint, angle variation, and wet finish. Floor and wall hits use the same sixteen generated deposits from **Floor Variants**. Each includes an albedo and coverage map and a nonmetallic roughness map. Pooled deposits also use a shallow relief normal. **Wall Drips** variants follow the surface's downhill direction with a small angle variation. The wall shuffles a pool per profile and uses every available texture before repeating, including a check against immediate repeats when reshuffling. Dense patches are smoother than their edges. **Floor Albedo Mix** controls how much receiver color remains under the deposit. Body hits alternate two generated cast-off patterns with matched roughness. **Wet Roughness** is the fallback for a pattern without a roughness texture. Oblique hits alternate rotation sides around the projected motion. Floor and wall rotation histories are tracked separately. Nearly head-on hits turn 60–150° from the previous offset. The deposit textures use 640 × 640 albedo and coverage maps with 384 × 384 roughness and relief maps. Their silhouettes came from three generated grayscale atlases. The supplied *Vermintide 2* footage and read-only study of its installed textures and VFX code informed the shape, buildup, and timing. [Deposit textures](../textures/effects/blood_deposits)

The player's **LensBlood** scene draws temporary drops and edge spray in the shared **ScreenEffects** canvas. The spray material samples a softened mip level of the atlas and feathers its luminance into alpha coverage. **Edge Tint** supplies the spray color and limits its opacity. Their centers are sampled on a ring **0.40–0.50** from screen center in normalized screen coordinates. Small drops slide down at an editable fraction of the viewport height per second, and the spray stays in place as it fades. The scene also owns burst timings, lifetimes, opacity, and the active mark cap. ## Damage vignette

**DamageVignette** draws over the ordinary scene vignette. Its hit shader multiplies the graded scene toward red using the alpha of an original **512 × 512** feathered mask. **Mask Size** sets the width and height of the centered texture in units of half the viewport diagonal. PlayerPresentation triggers the hit flash for positive damage received by the player. The standalone effect uses a **15 ms** rise and **185 ms** linear fade based on *Vermintide 2*'s incoming-hit particle. The saved Player instance uses a **1 s** fade. A new hit restarts the flash from its current strength. ## Damage direction HUD

**DamageIndicator** owns the authored style, layout, tint, body, and glow settings in its Inspector. The scene offers **Worn A1**, **Original A1**, **Classic**, **Ash Arc**, **Split Brush**, and **Torn Chevron**. The temporary editor preview draws one to eight markers in the active scene, adding them clockwise from the top at **45°** intervals. It uses the same tinted alpha mask and native bloom passes as a runtime hit. The translucent body draws into the HUD. **BloomViewport** draws emission over black in HDR 2D and runs Godot's **Canvas** environment glow in **Replace** mode. **BloomComposite** adds the resulting RGB over the main viewport, where the Playground uses **Sky**. The emission viewport resizes with the HUD and goes idle when there are no runtime markers or when the editor preview is off or leaves the active scene. The editor saves the scene with the bloom viewport idle.

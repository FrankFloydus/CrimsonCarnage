# Deep snow tracks

The saved [Snow Tracks demo](../addons/dynamic_sky/demo/snow_tracks_demo.tscn) runs the reference's 40 cm sphere around a 2 m radius at 85° per second. The sphere completes a lap in about 4.24 seconds. Open the scene and press **F6**. Use the saved **Grazing** camera as the current camera for a view across the track wall. The scene starts with an accumulated snow layer and uses a cold clear weather profile so airborne snow leaves the track visible.

The [UDW comparison](../addons/dynamic_sky/demo/udw_comparison/README.md) uses the same stamp and receiver on its landscape. Choose **Snow** or **Blizzard** to see the source sphere make its track. The receiver's **Track Region** limits the field to the finely subdivided 6.4 m inset around the orbit.

## Set up a receiver and a marker

1. Instance [Snow Track Receiver](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_receiver.tscn) in the area that needs deep tracks. Its saved example is a 6 × 6 m subdivided plane, a separate substrate collision body, and a Weather Surface material using the original snow PBR maps. To use a different mesh, assign the mesh and collision body and keep the mesh's local +Y aligned with world up. Author slopes in the vertices. Use roughly 2.5 cm vertex spacing where tracks need a clear silhouette. Set **Track Region** in the mesh's local X/Z coordinates when only part of a mesh receives tracks. An empty region uses the full mesh bounds.
2. Set the receiver's **Settings** resource for snow depth, compression, rim height, and sample spacing. Its defaults provide 14 cm of uncompressed snow and up to 80% compression. The existing **Weather Response** component supplies settled snow, including its authored exposure and response times. Roof collision and authored water regions reject stamps on covered or submerged receivers.
3. Instance [Snow Track Stamp](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_stamp.tscn) at a foot sole or rolling contact and assign its **Receiver**. Set the brush **Diameter Meters** and **Pressure** on the marker. Assign the actor's physics body if the contact rays should ignore it.
4. Enable **Continuous Contact** for a rolling or sliding marker. It samples up to 15 times a second and sweeps between valid contacts. For footsteps, leave it disabled and call `stamp_contact()` from an animation event. The marker queues that pose for the next physics tick.

Tracks move the rendered vertices and their lighting normals. **The substrate collision stays at its original height.** Actor movement and grounding still use that collision. The receiver needs more vertices than an ordinary material-only weather surface. Its field follows the receiving mesh, independent of the camera, and has a maximum of 512 samples per axis. Place separate receivers where local detail is needed.

**Settings → Normal Strength** controls how strongly track relief bends the shading normal. The supplied settings use **0.5** to soften the deep-snow wall lighting. **1.0** uses the full slope. Track depth, rim geometry, and the stored contact field retain their authored values at either setting. [Snow track settings](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_settings.tres)

**Retain Grazing Relief** uses the Unreal parallax material's reciprocal view-angle weighting to bring back lighting detail at low viewing angles. The supplied settings keep the authored strength above about 9° from the receiving surface and reach full relief at about 6.6°. A strength of **0.0** keeps the track lighting normals flat at every angle. The matched checks covered a 20.6° perspective orbit with a 23 cm rise, plus shallow and standalone snow receivers. [Track normal calculation](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_geometry.gdshaderinc)

Custom receiving shaders can include [Snow Track Geometry](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_geometry.gdshaderinc) and use its uniforms in their displacement and normal calculations. The receiver binds a local copy of the assigned material. [Comparison checker](../addons/dynamic_sky/demo/udw_comparison/materials/demo_checker.gdshader)

## Runtime behavior

The receiver binds one local two-channel texture. It stores compression and piled snow separately and batches uploads. A mark needs an upward-facing substrate contact and more than 2 cm of accumulated snow. The strongest local imprint supplies both the bowl and its raised edge at each sample. This keeps overlapping sections of a sweep from stacking rounded rims along the path. Spatially varying snow structure breaks up the bowl and lip. The comparison landscape uses the same channels to emphasize finer snowpack normals and roughness on packed snow and the pushed-up edge. Snowfall speeds gradual restoration, and melting removes the relief with the underlying snow layer. Call `clear_tracks()` to clear the marks while retaining the current accumulation.

On the custom Forward+ build, a visible receiver with **GI Mode → Static** refreshes its SDFGI contribution as the snow layer rises or melts. Requests during buildup are spaced at least 1.5 seconds apart and require a 2 cm change since the last request. A smaller final change is submitted after the layer settles and the refresh interval has passed. Stock builds continue rendering the displacement through the same receiver. [Receiver script](../addons/dynamic_sky/surfaces/snow_tracks/snow_track_receiver.gd)

The Unreal reference uses a 100 m scrolling field at 2.5 cm per texel and a continuous ground contact. Its stock landscape combines baseline vertex offset with parallax depth and height-gradient normals. The Godot receiver uses a bounded local field and real vertex displacement. Native Trail3D draws a standalone ribbon with its own width and lifetime. The receiver's contact marker supplies the collision-filtered points for the persistent height field.

# Original ridge gneiss material

`ridge_gneiss_outcrop.tres` is a separate material for the fractured ridge outcrop. It does not replace the existing alpine rock used by the mountains. Its diffuse source is original built-in ImageGen artwork in `masters/ridge_gneiss.png`, at its original **1254 × 1254** resolution. The source is retained without overwriting the previous rock master.

`bake_ridge_gneiss.py` writes five **4096 × 4096 derived storage maps** into `pbr/`: albedo, tangent normal, packed ORM, macro tangent normal, and macro ORM. Upsampling does not create native 4K photographic detail. The bake uses the project's existing periodic-boundary correction and wrapped resampling. The derived relief, AO, and roughness are conservative authored estimates from diffuse contrast, not measured scan channels.

The material uses the existing weather-surface shader. Its main world-space tile covers 6 m; the macro normal covers 18 m. The mesh supplies the large silhouette, joint openings, ridges, and sloping planes. Normal maps supply only smaller surface relief. Base and macro ORM maps store AO in R, roughness in G, and zero dielectric metalness in B. Snow and wet response reuse the existing shader and original snowpack maps. There are no emission or lighting gains.

The main editable controls are `world_texture_scale`, `normal_strength`, `macro_texture_scale`, and `macro_normal_strength`. Lower scale values make a tile cover more world meters. The maps are intended for world triplanar projection, so changing a boulder's object scale does not stretch the physical texture size.

## Generation prompt

The built-in ImageGen tool was used with this prompt:

> Use case: photorealistic-natural. Asset type: original seamless square PBR base-color source texture for a realistic alpine granite/gneiss rock outcrop in a game, viewed orthographically straight toward the rock surface. Produce a fully filled edge-to-edge stone texture, not a picture of a boulder or a landscape. At the represented scale this square covers about 6 by 6 meters of exposed bedrock. Show broad interlocking irregular fractured stone planes and naturally broken angular edges, with only 6 to 12 dominant large jointed areas across the square, each area having internally coherent pale-gray quartz/feldspar, charcoal mica and muted warm-gray mineral variation. Include sparse narrow quartz veins, gentle mineral foliation changing direction between fractured planes, a few iron-stained seams and very sparse dull olive lichen in old crevices. The broad geological structure must remain readable from a distance; retain fine realistic mineral grains when close. Natural granite/gneiss, convincingly photographic, restrained neutral gray and gray-brown with subtle varied greens in crevices. Lighting must be exceptionally flat and diffuse, neutral overcast cross-polarized material capture: base color only, no specular highlights, no strong directional shadows, no painted lighting or ambient shading. Texture must tile seamlessly on all four edges with no recognizable repeating central motif. Avoid evenly distributed gravel speckle, gravel piles, pebbles, cobblestones, repetitive horizontal ripples, wood-like stretched grain, cartoon cracks, thick black outlines, fabricated stylization, moss blanket, strong baked highlights, perspective, background, border, text, logos, and watermark. High detail square material source.
